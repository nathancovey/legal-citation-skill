# Case citations

## Anatomy of a full citation

```
State v. Bonner, 643 A.2d 13, 15 (Del. Super. Ct. 1995).
```

| Part | Here | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Case name | `State v. Bonner` | Italicized (or underlined) in practitioner style. The comma after it is **not**. |
| Volume | `643` | |
| Reporter | `A.2d` | Spacing matters — see below. |
| First page | `13` | The page the opinion begins on. |
| Pincite | `15` | The page you are relying on, after a comma. |
| Court and year | `(Del. Super. Ct. 1995)` | One parenthetical. |

## Pincites and page ranges

- Separate the first page and the pincite with a comma: `843 A.2d 136, 138`. Writing `843 A.2d 136–37` merges them into what looks like a range and drops the first page.
- If the material is on the first page, repeat it: `843 A.2d 136, 136`. A range that starts there: `843 A.2d 136, 136–37`.
- Ranges take an en dash (a hyphen is acceptable). Keep the last two digits and drop any other repeated digits: `871–72`, `217–18`, `1065–69`, `99–100`.
- Separate pages that aren't consecutive with commas: `567 A.2d at 871, 874`.
- A footnote: `573 A.3d at 217 n.11`.
- Never use "p." or "pp." in a citation.

## U.S. Supreme Court: one reporter only

Cite only U.S. Reports when the case appears there. Drop the parallel Supreme Court Reporter and Lawyers' Edition cites that database "copy with reference" features add:

- Wrong: `Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436, 444, [S. Ct. cite], [L. Ed. cite] (1966)`
- Right: `Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436, 444 (1966)`

Use `S. Ct.` only for recent decisions not yet in U.S. Reports.

## Westlaw and Lexis citations (unreported decisions)

```
<Case name>, No. <docket number>, <year> WL <number>, at *<page> (<court> <Mon. day, year>).
```

- Give the docket number as it appears in the opinion's caption. Databases sometimes strip its punctuation (`615CV1824ORL41GJK` for `6:15-cv-1824-Orl-41GJK`); keep the form consistent across your citations.
- The pincite is the database's star page, after `at`: `at *8`.
- The parenthetical takes the **full date** (abbreviated month, day, year), not just the year.
- Short form: `<Short name>, <year> WL <number>, at *<page>.` Then `Id. at *5.`
- When the first party is a government official, the short form uses the other party: `Sec'y of Lab. v. Smith Co.` becomes `Smith Co.`

## The court-and-year parenthetical

Name the court only to the extent the reporter doesn't already make it clear.

| Court | How it appears |
|---|---|
| U.S. Supreme Court, in U.S. Reports or the Supreme Court Reporter | Year only: `Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436, 444 (1966).` |
| Federal courts of appeals | `(3d Cir. 2006)`, `(D.C. Cir. 1992)`, `(Fed. Cir. 2015)` — ordinals are `2d` and `3d`, never `2nd`, `3rd`, `CA3`, or `C.C.A.` |
| Federal district courts | `(S.D.N.Y. 2010)`, `(D. Mass. 2015)`, `(N.D. Ill. 2008)` |
| A state's highest court | State only: `(Mich. 1999)`, not `(Mich. Sup. Ct. 1999)`. If the reporter is that state's official reporter for its highest court, give only the year. |
| Lower state courts | State and court: `(Del. Super. Ct. 1995)`, `(Pa. Super. Ct. 1992)` |
| Lower state courts whose reporter already names the court | Omit what the reporter conveys: a New York Appellate Division case in `N.Y.S.2d` is `(App. Div. 1985)`, not `(N.Y. App. Div. 1985)`. |

Add a department, district, or county only when it matters to the point.

**Never strip periods from a court abbreviation.** It is `N.Y.`, not `NY`. Some court databases store display names without periods. Adding missing punctuation can fix a citation; removing it never does.

## Reporter spacing

- Run adjacent single capitals together. Numbers and ordinals count as single capitals: `A.2d`, `F.3d`, `F.4th`, `N.W.2d`, `U.S.`, `S.D.N.Y.`, `N.Y.S.2d`.
- Put a space next to any abbreviation of two or more letters: `F. Supp. 2d`, `So. 2d`, `S. Ct.`, `L. Ed. 2d`, `Cal. App. 4th`, `F. App'x`, `D. Mass.`
- Wrong: `F.Supp.2d`, `F.App'x`, `So.2d`, `S.Ct.`, `L.Ed.2d`.

The common reporters are listed in `abbreviations.md`.

## Case names

Start from the name printed at the beginning of the opinion. Database captions and running headers are not in citation form — a Westlaw header may read `U.S. v. Brownlee`. Then:

- **Keep only the first party on each side.** Drop "et al.", additional parties, and alternative names.
- **Drop a leading "The"** from a party's name.
- **Procedural phrases:** `In re` (not "In the Matter of"), `ex rel.`, `Ex parte`.
- **States as parties:** drop "State of," "Commonwealth of," and "People of": `Doe v. Commonwealth of Pennsylvania` becomes `Doe v. Pennsylvania`. When a state prosecutes in its own courts, it appears simply as `State`, `Commonwealth`, or `People` — hence `State v. Bonner` in a Delaware court.
- **"United States" is never abbreviated when it is the whole party name:** `United States v. Brownlee`, never `U.S. v. Brownlee`. "U.S." is fine as part of a longer name: `U.S. Dep't of Agric.`
- **Spell out any geographic name that is a party's entire name:** `Delaware v. Prouse`, not `Del. v. Prouse`.

### Abbreviating words in a case name depends on where the name appears

| Where the name appears | What to abbreviate |
|---|---|
| **Inside a citation** — a citation sentence, citation clause, or footnote citation | Every word on the case-name list and every geographic term in `abbreviations.md`, unless the geographic term is the party's entire name. Other words of eight or more letters may be abbreviated if that saves real space and stays unambiguous. |
| **In a textual sentence** — the name is part of your prose | Only widely recognized initialisms (NAACP, FCC) and eight words: `&`, `Ass'n`, `Bros.`, `Co.`, `Corp.`, `Inc.`, `Ltd.`, `No.` Do not abbreviate even these when they are the first word of a party's name. |

The same case, two ways:

- Prose: In *Smith v. Department of Social Services*, the court held …
- Citation: *Smith v. Dep't of Soc. Servs.*, [volume] [reporter] [page] (…).

Form a plural by adding "s" to the abbreviation: `Servs.`, `Dep'ts`, `Ass'ns`.

## Prior and subsequent history

If the decision was later affirmed, reversed, or otherwise acted on, that history follows the citation (`aff'd`, `rev'd`, `cert. denied`). In practitioner style these explanatory phrases are italicized. Check a full style guide for when history must be included.

## Errors found in real drafts

| As written | Correct | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| `State v. Andrews, 843 A.2d 136–37 (Del. Super. Ct. 2003)` | `843 A.2d 136, 136–37` | First page and pincite merged |
| `Bonner, 643 at 15` | `Bonner, 643 A.2d at 15` | Reporter missing from short form |
| `U.S. v. Brownlee` | `United States v. Brownlee` | Party name abbreviated (copied from a database header) |
| `(NY 1977)` | `(N.Y. 1977)` | Periods stripped from the court |
| `(Mich. Sup. Ct. 1999)` | `(Mich. 1999)` | Highest court named unnecessarily |
| `F.Supp.2d` | `F. Supp. 2d` | Spacing |

# Typeface, placement, and capitalization

The Bluebook has two sets of conventions:

- **The Bluepages** (B1–B23) for briefs, motions, memos, and opinions, which this skill calls practitioner style.
- **The Whitepages** (Rules 1–23, especially Rule 2) for law review articles and notes, which it calls academic style.

Nearly everything else in the citation is the same. Know which one the document uses before you change any typeface. If you can't tell, ask (see `SKILL.md`).

## At a glance (B2, Rule 2)

| | Bluepages: brief, memo | Whitepages: law review |
|---|---|---|
| Where citations go | In the text, right after what they support. Footnotes only if local rules allow (B1.1). | In footnotes. Citations never appear in the main text (Rule 1.1). |
| Case name, full citation | *Italic* or underlined | Roman, except procedural phrases (*In re*, *ex rel.*), which stay italic |
| Case name, short form | *Italic* | *Italic* |
| Case name in a textual sentence | *Italic* | *Italic* |
| Signals, *id.*, *supra*, *aff'd* and other history phrases | *Italic* | *Italic* |
| Book author and title | Roman author, *italic title* | Large-and-small capitals for both |
| Article title | *Italic* | *Italic* |
| Periodical name | Roman | Large-and-small capitals |
| Codes, statutes, rules, restatements | Roman | Large-and-small capitals for the code or rules name (Rule 12.3, Rule 12.9) |
| Small capitals | Never required (B2) | Required where listed |

Example of the same material in each style (Rule 2):

> **Brief:** Directors manage the business and affairs of a corporation. *See Revlon, Inc. v. MacAndrews & Forbes Holdings, Inc.*, 506 A.2d 173, 179 (Del. 1986); *see also* Del. Code Ann. tit. 8, § 141(a) (2000). In *Guth v. Loft*, the court held that directors also owe a duty of loyalty to the shareholders. 5 A.2d 503, 510 (Del. 1939).
>
> **Law review:** Directors manage the business and affairs of a corporation.¹ In *Guth v. Loft*, the court held that directors also owe a duty of loyalty to the shareholders.²
> ¹ *See* Revlon, Inc. v. MacAndrews & Forbes Holdings, Inc., 506 A.2d 173, 179 (Del. 1986); *see also* DEL. CODE ANN. tit. 8, § 141(a) (2000) (providing statutory basis).
> ² 5 A.2d 503, 510 (Del. 1939).

(DEL. CODE ANN. stands for large-and-small capitals.)

## Bluepages: briefs, motions, memos (B2, B7)

Italicize **or** underline the following. Pick one and use it throughout (B2, Bluepages introduction).

- Case names, full and short, including `v.` and procedural phrases (`In re`, `ex rel.`)
- Signals: *See*, *See also*, *Cf.*, *But see*, *See, e.g.,* (everything except the final comma)
- Explanatory phrases introducing case history: *aff'd*, *rev'd*, *cert. denied*, *overruled by*
- *Id.* and *supra*
- Phrases introducing related authority: *quoted in*, *cited in*
- Titles of books and articles, and of congressional hearings
- In your prose: publication titles, words italicized in the original of a quotation, words you emphasize, and uncommon foreign words

Leave these in roman type:

- volume, reporter, pages, and the court-and-year parenthetical
- **the comma after a case name**: `State v. Bonner, 643 A.2d …` (italics stop before the comma)
- the words "quoting" and "citing" inside a parenthetical (Rule 2.1(e))
- a comma after a history phrase: *aff'd*, then a roman comma
- ellipses and brackets, even inside an italicized quotation (Rule 2.1(g))
- statute, rule, regulation, and constitution citations, entirely (B12.1.1 tip)
- a signal used as a verb in your sentence: "For a full discussion, see …" (Rule 1.2(e))
- Latin words in common legal use (Rule 7(b)): *e.g.* as a signal is italic, but in prose these are roman: e.g., i.e., res judicata, prima facie, mens rea, certiorari, habeas corpus, amicus curiae, en banc, de jure, quid pro quo. Obsolete or uncommon Latin phrases stay italic: *expressio unius est exclusio alterius*.

Punctuation that falls *inside* the italicized material is italicized with it. That is why **the period in *Id.* is italicized**: it's part of the abbreviation (B10.2 tip, Rule 4.1).

## Whitepages: law review footnotes (Rule 2)

- In a **full** citation in a footnote, the case name is **roman**, but procedural phrases stay italic: State *ex rel.* Scott v. Zinn.
- **Short-form** case names, signals, *Id.*, and explanatory phrases are **italic**.
- A case name used **grammatically** in a sentence, whether in the main text or a footnote, is **italic**. One in a citation clause embedded in a footnote sentence follows citation conventions, so it is roman in full form (Rule 2.2(b)(i)).
- Books: authors and titles in large-and-small capitals. Periodicals: article title italic, journal name in large-and-small capitals, author roman. Follow the publication's own style sheet where it differs.
- A case name inside an article title in a citation is **not** italicized, even when it is the whole title (Rule 2.1(a)).
- **Footnote call numbers** go after any punctuation mark except a dash or a colon: "sentence one.¹", "this one³ — is surprising", "one thing⁴:" (Rule 1.1(a)). Put the call at the end of the sentence if the authority supports the whole sentence, or right after the part it supports.
- Short forms in footnotes follow the five-footnote rule (`short-forms.md`). *Supra* cross-references give a note number: `Reich, supra note 16, at 6.` (Rule 4.2(a)).

## Checking typeface in a word processor

Italics that look right on screen are often slightly wrong in the file:

- the emphasis stops one character early, or covers a trailing space,
- the emphasis runs onto the comma after the case name,
- the period in *Id.* is left roman.

Check those three spots specifically.

## Citation sentences and citation clauses (B1.1, Rule 1.1)

| Form | Supports | Mechanics |
|---|---|---|
| **Citation sentence** | The whole preceding sentence | Follows that sentence; starts with a capital letter, ends with a period. Several citations in one sentence are separated by semicolons. |
| **Citation clause** | Only part of a sentence | Set off by commas right after the part it supports; lowercase unless the first word is otherwise capitalized; no period unless it ends the sentence. |

Both are correct. Prefer citation sentences when you can, because clauses break up the prose.

**A split citation is also correct.** You can name the case in your sentence and give the rest of the citation in the citation sentence that follows. Because the name is in the text, the citation may leave it out (Rule 10.2.2; it is the Bluebook's own example in Rule 2):

> In *State v. Bonner*, the court held that being placed in a patrol car does not by itself make an encounter prolonged. 643 A.2d 13, 15 (Del. Super. Ct. 1995).

Embedding the rest of the citation in the sentence, set off by commas, is equally correct (B10.1.1(vi)): "In *State v. Bonner*, 643 A.2d 13 (Del. Super. Ct. 1995), the court held …"

**Never report placement as an error.** A reviewer that "fixes" a valid split citation by adding the name back, or moves a citation sentence into a clause, creates churn. It can also contradict itself across two comments on the same citation.

In court documents and memos, citations go in the text, not in footnotes, unless local rules say otherwise (B1.1). Record citations may also be enclosed in parentheses (`court-documents.md`).

## Capitalization in your text (B8, Rule 8)

- **"Court":** capitalize it when naming any court in full, when referring to the U.S. Supreme Court, and, in a court document, when referring to the court that will receive it. Otherwise lowercase: "This Court has already ruled," but "the court in *Watkins*," and "the court of appeals reversed."
- **Party designations** ("Plaintiff," "Defendant," "Appellant," "Appellee"): capitalize only for the parties in your own matter. "Plaintiff denies Defendant's allegations," but "in *Smith*, the plaintiffs alleged."
- **Court-document titles:** capitalize the actual title of a document filed in your matter, and don't abbreviate it in text. Lowercase a generic reference: "the initial temporary restraining order" (`court-documents.md`).
- **Other words** (Rule 8(d)): capitalize "Act" for a specific act, "Circuit" with its name or number ("the Fifth Circuit"), "Constitution" for any constitution named in full and for the U.S. Constitution (but "constitutional" is lowercase), "State" and "Commonwealth" when the state is a party or acting as a government ("The State brought this action"), and "Justice" or "Judge" with a name.
- **Headings and titles:** capitalize every word except articles, conjunctions, and prepositions of four or fewer letters, unless one begins the heading or follows a colon (Rule 8(a)).

## Judges (B9, Rule 9)

In text: "Justice Story," "Chief Justice Roberts," "Judge Hand," "Chief Judge Friendly." No first names unless two judges on the court share a surname. In parentheticals: `(Holmes, J.)`, `(Roberts, C.J.)`, `(Black & White, JJ.)`.

## Spacing

One space after a period or other punctuation in a proportional font. Two spaces only with a monospaced font such as Courier (Rule 2.3).

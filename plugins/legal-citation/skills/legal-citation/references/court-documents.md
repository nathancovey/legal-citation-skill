# Court documents and the record

Citations to the record, pleadings, motions, briefs, depositions, affidavits, transcripts, and exhibits. Bluebook B17 and table BT1 govern documents filed in the case you are writing in. Rule 10.8.3 governs documents filed in a different case.

The first principle applies here too. A record page, a transcript line, an exhibit letter, and a docket number are all numbers. Copy each one from the document. If you can't see it, write `___`.

## Which rule applies

| You are citing | Rule | Looks like |
|---|---|---|
| A document filed in **this** case: the record, the complaint, an affidavit, the other side's brief | B17 | `Def.'s Mot. Summ. J. 2, Dkt. No. 15.` |
| A document filed in **another** case | Rule 10.8.3 | `Brief for Petitioner at 24, Scott v. Harris, 550 U.S. 372 (2007) (No. 05-1631).` |

## Full citation (B17.1)

It has up to four parts, in this order:

1. the document's title, abbreviated with BT1;
2. a pinpoint;
3. the date, only when B17.1.3 calls for it;
4. the electronic docket number, if the document was filed electronically (B17.1.4).

No case name, court, or year. The reader knows which case it is.

### Abbreviate the title (B17.1.1, BT1)

- Abbreviate words in the title using the table below. A word of seven or more letters that isn't listed may be abbreviated if the result is unambiguous.
- Drop articles and prepositions unless that would confuse the reader. Other extra words may go too, if the reader can still tell which document it is.
- Where there is an official record, such as the Record on Appeal, **always** write `R.`
- Some words are **never** abbreviated. They are marked "do not abbreviate" in the table.
- Name an affiant, declarant, or deponent by last name: `Hawkins Aff.`, `Clark Dep.`, `Travis Decl.`

> ✓ `Def.'s Mem. Supp. Summ. J. 6, 9.`
> ✓ `Pls.' Am. Answer to Def.'s Countercl. 3–4.`
> ✗ `Def.'s Mot. Dism. 3.` → ✓ `Def.'s Mot. Dismiss 3.` ("Dismiss" is never abbreviated.)
> ✗ `Defendant's Motion for Summary Judgment 2.` → ✓ `Def.'s Mot. Summ. J. 2.`

**Never abbreviate a document's title in a textual sentence** (B8):

> ✓ For all of the above reasons, Appellant's Petition for Rehearing ought to be granted.
> ✗ For all of the above reasons, Appellant's Pet. Reh'g ought to be granted.

### Pinpoints (B17.1.2)

- Cite the precise spot. Never use "p."
- **Pages take no "at"**, except in record citations, where "at" is customary: `R. at 9.` Also use ", at" after an exhibit or volume designation that would otherwise run into the page number (Rule 3.2(a)): `Travis Decl. Ex. B, at 3.`
- **Transcripts and depositions:** page and line, separated by a colon: `Clark Dep. 15:21–16:4.` A range across pages repeats the page.
- Identify other subdivisions with their symbol or abbreviation: `Compl. ¶ 7.`, `Def.'s Aff. ¶¶ 5–7.`, `Trial Tr. vol. 2, 31.`
- Use commas only to avoid confusion.

> ✓ `Pet'r's Br. 6.`  ✗ `Pet'r's Br. at 6.`  ✗ `Pet'r's Br. p. 6.`
> ✓ `R. at 9.`
> ✓ `Clark Dep. 15:21–16:4.`  ✗ `Clark Dep. 15, lines 21–25.`
> ✓ `Travis Decl. Ex. B, at 3.`  ✗ `Travis Decl. Ex. B 3.`

### Dates (B17.1.3)

Give the date only when:

1. more than one document has the same title,
2. the date is relevant to the discussion, or
3. the date is needed to avoid confusion.

The date follows the pinpoint, after a comma:

> ✓ `Perryman Dep. 34:15–18, Aug. 7, 2002; Trial Tr. vol. 2, 51–52, Dec. 12, 2002.`
> ✓ `Beatrice Aff. ¶ 9, May 10, 2003.` (She filed two affidavits.)
> ✗ A date on every citation when none of the three conditions applies.

Abbreviate months per table T12 (in `abbreviations.md`).

### Electronic docket numbers (B17.1.4)

When a document was filed through PACER/ECF or a state e-filing system, add its docket number at the end:

> ✓ `Def.'s Mot. Summ. J. 2, Dkt. No. 15.`
> ✓ `Feder Dep. 5:30–12:10, Dkt. No. 6.`

- Copy the number from the ECF header or the docket. Never guess it from the order of filings.
- **Pages:** if the ECF header's page numbers differ from the document's own page numbers, cite the document's own numbers (B17.1.4 tip).
- Many courts prescribe their own form, such as `ECF No. 15`. Local rules win (BT2).
- A closed-universe assignment record often has no docket numbers. Then there is nothing to add. Don't invent one.

## Parentheses around court-document citations (B17.1.1 tip)

Citations to court documents **may** be enclosed in parentheses. It's optional. Decide once for the document and do the same for every court-document citation, including *Id.*

When you use them, the parentheses enclose the whole citation sentence, **period included**. The B17 example: `(Def.'s Resp. to Pl.'s Interrog. No. 3.)`

> ✓ The officers told her she could leave once the paperwork was done. (R. at 12.)
> ✗ The officers told her she could leave once the paperwork was done (R. at 12).
> ✗ The officers told her she could leave once the paperwork was done. (R. at 12).
> ✓ Without parentheses: The officers told her she could leave once the paperwork was done. R. at 12.
> ✗ Mixed: `(R. at 12.)` in one paragraph and `R. at 14.` in the next.

A citation that supports only part of a sentence is a citation clause, set off by commas, not parentheses (B1.1): `Petitioner admits filing suit more than one year later, Pet'r's Br. 6, and further admits the one-year limit applies, Pet'r's Br. 7.`

The tip covers court documents only. Case and statute citations stay ordinary citation sentences and clauses (B1.1), even in a brief that puts its record citations in parentheses.

## Short forms and *Id.* (B17.2)

After a full citation, use a short form when the reader will know what you mean, the full citation is in the same general discussion, and it is easy to find. A short form may drop the docket number and shorten the title:

| First reference | Later |
|---|---|
| `App. to Pet. Cert. 137–39, Dkt. No. 15.` | `App. 137–39, Dkt. No. 15.` or `App. 137–39.` |

**Use *Id.* for a court document only when it saves significant space** (B17.2 tip):

> ✓ `Leach Aff. 33–39.` … `Id. at 52.` (*Id.* replaces a longer title.)
> ✓ `R. at 5.` … `R. at 12.`
> ✗ `R. at 5.` … `Id. at 12.` (*Id.* saves nothing over `R.`)

- *Id.* takes "at" before a page, like any *Id.*: `Id. at 52.`, and is italicized with its period.
- *Id.* still needs a one-authority predecessor (B4). After `R. at 5; Leach Aff. 33.`, *Id.* is wrong.
- If your record citations use parentheses, so does *Id.*: `(Id. at 52.)`, with only `Id.` italic.

## Documents from another case (Rule 10.8.3)

- Give the document's full title as it appears on the filing, abbreviated only per Rule 10.2.1(c) (the eight words: `&`, `Ass'n`, `Bros.`, `Co.`, `Corp.`, `Inc.`, `Ltd.`, `No.`). BT1 abbreviations are for documents in your own case.
- Pinpoint with "at", then the case citation, then the docket number in parentheses.
- If the case has no decision yet, the parenthetical date is the filing date, and the docket number is part of the citation itself.
- **Always include the docket number.** An ECF document number is optional unless needed to find the document.
- A parallel Westlaw or Lexis citation is optional, but if you give one, it needs its own pinpoint.

> ✓ `Brief for Petitioner at 24, Scott v. Harris, 550 U.S. 372 (2007) (No. 05-1631).`
> ✓ `Plaintiff's Motion for Summary Judgment at 2, In re 3M Combat Arms Earplug Prods. Liab. Litig., No. 20-CV-47213 (N.D. Fla. July 7, 2022).`
> ✓ `Transcript of Oral Argument at 11, Ayers v. Belmontes, 127 S. Ct. 469 (2006) (No. 05-493).`
> ✗ `Pet'r's Br. 24, Scott v. Harris, 550 U.S. 372 (2007).` (BT1 abbreviations, no "at", and no docket number.)

In a brief or memo, italicize the case name inside these citations (B2).

## Capitalization in your text (B8)

- Capitalize a document's title only when it was filed in this matter **and** you use its actual title or a shortened form of it: "In their Memorandum of Points and Authorities in Opposition to Defendants' Motion to Dismiss, Plaintiffs argue …" A generic reference stays lowercase: "the initial temporary restraining order."
- Capitalize "Plaintiff," "Defendant," "Appellant," and "Appellee" only for the parties to this matter: "Plaintiff denies Defendant's allegations," but "In *Smith*, the plaintiffs alleged …"
- Capitalize "Court" for the court that will receive the document, or when naming any court in full: "This Court has already ruled," but "The court in *Watkins* was attempting …"

## Check the sequence

Record citations are positional like any short form. In document order, flag:

- parentheses used for some court-document citations and not others,
- a period outside the closing parenthesis, or a parenthesized citation inside the sentence it supports,
- `Id.` for the record or another short title where it saves no space,
- "at" before the page of a document other than the record (`Pet'r's Br. at 6`), or "p.",
- a deposition or transcript cite without page:line,
- a date added where B17.1.3 doesn't call for it, or missing where two documents share a title,
- a docket number missing from an electronically filed document, or one you can't trace to the docket,
- an abbreviated document title in a textual sentence.

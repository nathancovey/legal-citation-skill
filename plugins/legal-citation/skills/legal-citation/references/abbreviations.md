# Abbreviations, numerals, and symbols

Use the word lists when abbreviating inside a **citation**. In a textual sentence, abbreviate far less: see `case-citations.md`. Court-document titles have their own list (BT1) in `court-documents.md`.

The lists started from the Free Law Project's `reporters-db` (BSD 3-Clause; notice in `LICENSE-reporters-db.txt`). They have been corrected and extended against the Bluebook's tables T1, T6, T7, T8, T10, T12, and T16 (22d ed. 2025).

## Spacing and periods (B6, Rule 6.1)

- **Close up adjacent single capitals.** Numerals and ordinals count as single capitals: `U.S.`, `N.W.`, `S.D.N.Y.`, `F.3d`, `S.E.2d`, `A.L.R.4th`.
- **Space next to any abbreviation of two or more letters:** `S. Ct.`, `F. Supp. 2d`, `So. 2d`, `Cal. App. 3d`, `D. Mass.`
- **Exception for court filings (B6, B10.1.2):** in a document filed with a court that has a word limit, reporter abbreviations may be closed up to save words: `S.Ct.`, `F.Supp.2d`. It's optional. Use one form throughout. In a memo or law review, use the spaced form. Don't flag `F.Supp.2d` in a brief that uses it consistently.
- **Every abbreviation takes a period**, except one whose last letter is set off by an apostrophe: `Univ.`, `Bldg.`, but `Ass'n`, `Dep't`, `Soc'y`.
- **Entities known by their initials in speech** (`FCC`, `NLRB`, `EPA`, `NAACP`, `AARP`) drop the periods in text, case names, and institutional authors: `City of Arlington v. FCC`. **Never drop periods from a reporter, a code, or a court:** `F.C.C.` as a reporter, `N.Y.`, `S.D.` (Rule 6.1(b)).
- Abbreviations not in the Bluebook's tables are avoided unless they save substantial space and stay unambiguous (Rule 6.1).

> ✗ `F.Supp. 2d`, `So.2d`, `L.Ed.2d` in a memo  ✓ `F. Supp. 2d`, `So. 2d`, `L. Ed. 2d`
> ✗ `(NY 1977)`  ✓ `(N.Y. 1977)`

## Numerals and symbols (B6, Rule 6.2)

| Rule | ✓ | ✗ |
|---|---|---|
| Spell out zero to ninety-nine in text. Use numerals for 100 and up. | "forty-two days," "117 days" | "42 days" |
| Spell out any number that begins a sentence. | "Twenty-one plaintiffs …" | "21 plaintiffs …" |
| A series with numbers both under and over 100 uses numerals throughout. | "117, 6, and 28 pounds" | "117, six, and twenty-eight pounds" |
| Numerals for decimals, for repeated percentages or dollar amounts, and for section numbers. | "3.5 miles," "a 6% rate" | |
| Commas in numbers of four or more digits, **except** pages, volumes, docket numbers, database numbers, and U.S. Code sections. | "1,234,567", `2003 WL 131711`, `at 1065` | `2003 WL 131,711` |
| Ordinals in citations use `2d` and `3d`. In text, use "2nd" and "3rd". Never superscripts. | `103d Cong.`, `2d Cir.`; "the 102nd Congress" | `103rd Cong.`, `2nd Cir.` |
| `§` and `¶` in citations, with a space before the number. Spell out "section" and "paragraph" in text, except for a U.S. Code or federal regulation provision. | `§ 1983`; "section 1701 of the Delaware Code" | `§1983` |
| `$` and `%` wherever numerals are used, with no space. Never begin a sentence with a symbol. | "$10,000," "6%" | "$ 10,000," "6 %" |

## Case-name words (T6)

How to use the list (T6, Rule 10.2.2):

- **In citations, abbreviate every listed word, even when it is the first word of a party's name**: `S. Consol. R.R. v. Consol. Transp. Co.`, `McGaugh v. Comm'r`. The only exception is a geographic unit that is a party's entire name (see States).
- Words not listed stay spelled out, except that a word of **eight or more letters** may be abbreviated if that saves substantial space and the result is unambiguous.
- **Plurals add "s"** to the abbreviation unless shown otherwise: `Servs.`, `Dep'ts`, `Ass'ns`, `Schs.`, `Pharms.`
- **Possessives:** singular `Emp.'s`, plural `Emps.'`.
- Some abbreviations look alike. `Lab.` is Labor, but Laboratory is `Lab'y`. `Ref.` is Referee, but Refining is `Refin.` `Dig.` is Digest, but Digital is `Digit.`

## States, territories, and cities (T10)

Abbreviate a geographic unit when it is **part of** a party's name or appears in a court designation. When it is a party's **entire** name, spell it out: `Delaware v. Prouse`, `Staub v. District of Columbia`, `Fritz v. County of Los Angeles`, `Alvarez-Machain v. United States`. But `Mia. Herald v. Sercus`, `LeBeau v. Univ. of Md.`, `In re W. Tex. Pepper Co.`

Courts get renamed. Maryland's intermediate court has been the Appellate Court of Maryland (`Md. App. Ct.`) since December 14, 2022. Before that it was the Court of Special Appeals (`Md. Ct. Spec. App.`). Use the name the court had when it decided the case.

App.` A recent opinion of that court is a useful check. **Never remove periods** from an abbreviation you find.

## Explanatory phrases for case history (T8)

All are italicized. A comma after the phrase is roman. Phrases marked "no comma" take their citation directly.

*acq.* · *acq. in result* · *aff'd* · *aff'd by an equally divided court* · *aff'd mem.* · *aff'd on other grounds* · *aff'd on reh'g* · *aff'g* (no comma) · *amended by* (no comma) · *appeal denied* · *appeal dismissed* · *appeal docketed* · *appeal filed* · *argued* · *cert. denied* · *cert. dismissed* · *cert. dismissed as improvidently granted* · *cert. granted* · *certifying question to* (no comma) · *dismissing appeal from* (no comma) · *enforced* · *enforcing* (no comma) · *invalidated by* (no comma) · *mandamus denied* · *mandate held* · *modified* · *modifying* (no comma) · *nonacq.* · *overruled by* (no comma) · *perm. app. denied* · *perm. app. granted* · *petition for cert. filed* · *prob. juris. noted* · *reh'g granted* · *reh'g denied* · *rev'd* · *rev'd on other grounds* · *rev'd summarily* · *rev'g* (no comma) · *vacated* · *vacating as moot* (no comma) · *withdrawn*

The rules also use *sub nom.* (`rev'd sub nom.`), *rev'd en banc*, *aff'd per curiam*, *abrogated by*, *superseded by statute*, and *as recognized in* (Rule 10.7). When to use them is in `case-citations.md`.

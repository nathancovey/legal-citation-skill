# Abbreviation lists

Use these when abbreviating inside a **citation**. In a textual sentence, abbreviate far less — see `case-citations.md`.

The case-name, state, and reporter lists are derived from the Free Law Project's `reporters-db` (BSD 3-Clause; notice in `LICENSE-reporters-db.txt`).

## Court designations

| Court | Example |
|---|---|
| Federal court of appeals (numbered) | `(2d Cir. 2001)`, `(3d Cir. 2006)`, `(11th Cir. 2019)` |
| D.C. and Federal Circuits | `(D.C. Cir. 1992)`, `(Fed. Cir. 2015)` |
| Federal district court | `(D. Del. 2012)`, `(S.D.N.Y. 2010)`, `(N.D. Ill. 2008)`, `(E.D. Pa. 2004)`, `(C.D. Cal. 2017)` |
| Bankruptcy court | `(Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 1992)` |
| State highest court | state only: `(Mich. 1999)` |
| State intermediate or trial court | `(Del. Super. Ct. 1995)`, `(Pa. Super. Ct. 1992)`, `(Cal. Ct. App. 2005)`, `(Tex. Crim. App. 2010)` |
| Court already named by the reporter | `(App. Div. 1985)` in `N.Y.S.2d` |

Directions in federal district names are single capitals and run together: `S.D.N.Y.`, `N.D. Ill.` (the space comes before the multi-letter state). Ordinals are `2d` and `3d`, not `2nd` and `3rd`.

Court abbreviations vary by jurisdiction. When the court isn't on this list, copy the form from a recent opinion of that court or the user's style guide — and never remove periods from an abbreviation you find.

# Short citations and Id.

## When you may shorten

After citing a case in full, later citations to it may use a short form when all three hold:

1. the reader can tell which case you mean,
2. the full citation appears in the same general discussion, and
3. the reader can find the full citation without trouble.

In a long memo or brief, if the case hasn't appeared for several pages or you have started a new major section, repeating the full citation is the safer choice. That is a judgment call, not a rule.

## Short-form patterns

| Form | When |
|---|---|
| `Bonner, 643 A.2d at 15.` | The standard short form. |
| `643 A.2d at 15.` | Your sentence already names the case, so the reader has no doubt which one. |
| `Id. at 15.` | Same case as the immediately preceding citation, different page. |
| `Id.` | Same case **and** same page or pages as the immediately preceding citation. |
| `Bonner, 643 A.2d 13.` | The whole case rather than a particular page: first page, no pincite, no parenthetical. |

Every short form that points to a page uses "at".

## Which party name to use

Use the first party's name unless it is a government, a geographic unit, an official, or another routine litigant ("State," "United States," "People," "Commonwealth," a city, "Commissioner"). In that case use the other party.

- `State v. Bonner` → `Bonner`
- `United States v. Brownlee` → `Brownlee`

A long name may be shortened if the result is still unambiguous. Italicize the short name in both styles. The comma after it is not italicized.

## Id.

- **Id.** refers to the immediately preceding authority, and only when that citation contained exactly one authority. After `See Coyle, 567 A.2d at 873; Bonner, 643 A.2d at 15.`, the next citation cannot be `Id.` — the reader can't tell which case it means.
- Authorities that appear only inside a parenthetical (`(quoting …)`, `(citing …)`) or in prior or subsequent history don't count toward that rule.
- `Id.` alone means the same page or pages. For a new page: `Id. at 874.`
- Capitalize it only when it starts a citation sentence: `Id. at 874.` In a citation clause or after a signal it is lowercase: `see id. at 874`, `See id.`
- **The period is part of the abbreviation and is italicized (or underlined) with it.**
- In academic footnotes, `Id.` may also refer to the previous footnote if that footnote cited exactly one authority.
- Do not use "supra" for cases.

## Check the sequence

Short forms are positional, so check them in document order. Track the immediately preceding authority as you go.

```
… State v. Coyle, 567 A.2d 870, 873 (Del. Super. Ct. 1989).      full citation
… Coyle, 567 A.2d at 871–72.                                       short form
… Id.                                                              still 871–72
… Id. at 874.                                                      new page
… Hernandez, 573 A.3d at 218.                                      different case: no Id.
… Coyle, 567 A.2d at 874.                                          back to Coyle: short form, not Id.
```

Flag:

- an `Id.` that follows a citation containing more than one authority,
- an `Id.` that follows a citation to a different case,
- a short form for a case that has no earlier full citation,
- a short form missing its reporter (`Bonner, 643 at 15`) or its "at".

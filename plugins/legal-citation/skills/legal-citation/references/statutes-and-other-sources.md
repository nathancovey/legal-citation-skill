# Statutes and other sources

Constitutions, statutes, court rules, restatements, regulations, legislative materials, books, articles, websites, and AI output, in the practitioner form of the Bluepages (B11–B19) with the Whitepages rules behind them. For court documents and the record, see `court-documents.md`.

The first principle is the same as for cases. A title number, section, year of a code volume, or page of a report is copied from the source, never supplied from memory.

## What is italicized

In a brief or memo (B2): titles of books and articles, titles of congressional hearings, signals, *id.*, *supra*, and case names. **Nothing in a statute, rule, regulation, or constitution citation is italicized** (B12.1.1 tip). Small capitals, which the Whitepages use for codes and books, are never required in practitioner documents (B2).

> ✓ 42 U.S.C. § 1983.  ✗ *42 U.S.C. § 1983.*

## Sections and paragraphs (Rule 3.3, Rule 6.2(c))

- One space after `§` and `¶`: `§ 1983`, not `§1983`. Two symbols for more than one: `§§ 9601–9675`, `¶¶ 4471–4474`.
- Give inclusive numbers. **Never use "et seq."**
- Keep the source's own punctuation between section and subsection: `N.M. Stat. Ann. § 4-44-7(G)`. If it has none, use parentheses: `(1)(a)`.
- Several subsections of one section take one symbol: `19 U.S.C. § 1485(a)(1)–(3)`. Subsections of different sections take two: `19 U.S.C. §§ 1485(a), 1486(b)`.
- If a dash would be ambiguous, use "to": `42 U.S.C. §§ 1973aa-2 to -4`.
- **No "at" before § or ¶:** ✓ `Id. § 1332.` ✗ `Id. at § 1332.`
- In a textual sentence, spell out "section," except for a U.S. Code or federal regulation provision, and never start a sentence with `§` (Rule 6.2(c), Rule 12.10(c)).

> ✓ Section 1983 provides a remedy. Plaintiffs sue under § 1983. The claim arises under section 1701 of the Delaware Code.
> ✗ § 1983 provides a remedy.

## Constitutions (B11, Rule 11)

> ✓ U.S. Const. art. I, § 8, cl. 10.
> ✓ U.S. Const. amend. XIV, § 2.
> ✓ Wash. Const. art. I, § 32.

- No date for a provision in force. A repealed or amended provision: `U.S. Const. amend. XVIII (repealed 1933).`
- **No short form except *id.***: ✓ `Id. amend. XIV, § 1.` ✗ `U.S. Const., supra, amend. XIV.` ✗ `Const. amend. XIV.`
- In a textual sentence, capitalize the parts of the U.S. Constitution: "Article IV, Section 1," "the Fourteenth Amendment," "the Full Faith and Credit Clause." In citations they stay abbreviated and lowercase: `art. IV, § 1` (Rule 8(d)(ii), Rule 11).

## Statutes (B12, Rule 12)

### Federal

| What | Form |
|---|---|
| Code section | `42 U.S.C. § 1983.` |
| With the act's name | `Administrative Procedure Act § 6, 5 U.S.C. § 555.` (`§ 6` is the act's own section; `§ 555` is the Code's.) |
| Unofficial code | `15 U.S.C.A. § 205 (West).` The publisher is required. |
| Session law | `Department of Transportation Act, Pub. L. No. 89-670, § 9, 80 Stat. 931, 944–47 (1966).` |

- The year may be omitted for the U.S. Code, official or unofficial (B12.1.1, Rule 12.3.2). If you give it, copy it from the volume: the spine, then the title page, then the copyright year.
- Give the act's name only if the statute is commonly cited that way or the name helps identify it (Rule 12.3.1(a)). Omit a leading "The."
- Cite the session laws for the historical fact of enactment, or when the statute isn't in the code yet (B12.1.1, Rule 12.2.2(b)). Omit the year parenthetical if the act's name already contains its year.
- The Internal Revenue Code may be cited `I.R.C. § 61` instead of `26 U.S.C. § 61` (B12.1.4).

### State

Cite the official code if there is one (table T1.3 lists it first). Give the year of the code edition, **not** the year the act passed, and the publisher for an unofficial code:

> ✓ Wash. Rev. Code § 28B.20.020 (2014).
> ✓ Cal. Penal Code § 181 (West 2011).
> ✓ N.Y. Bus. Corp. Law § 717 (McKinney 2000).
> ✗ The year the statute was enacted, in place of the code volume's year.

The form varies by state: some use titles (`Del. Code Ann. tit. 13, § 1301`), some name the subject (`Cal. Veh. Code`). Copy the pattern from T1.3, a recent opinion of that state's courts, or the state's own citation rule (BT2). A missing year is `___`, not a guess.

### Short forms (B12.2, Rule 12.10)

After a full citation, any short form that clearly identifies the statute works in the same general discussion:

| Full | Short |
|---|---|
| `42 U.S.C. § 1983` | `§ 1983` or `42 U.S.C. § 1983` |
| `Administrative Procedure Act § 1, 5 U.S.C. § 551` | `§ 1`, `5 U.S.C. § 551`, or `Administrative Procedure Act § 1` |
| `Del. Code Ann. tit. 28, § 1701 (1999)` | `tit. 28, § 1701` or `§ 1701` |
| `Cal. Educ. Code § 48222 (West 2008)` | `Educ. § 48222` |

*Id.* works for a statute or regulation **in the same title** as the one just cited: `28 U.S.C. § 1331.` … `Id. § 1332.` After `28 U.S.C. § 1331`, a cite to `42 U.S.C. § 1983` can't be *id.*

Never use *supra* for statutes (B4).

## Court rules, restatements, uniform acts, guidelines (B12.1.3, Rule 12.9)

> ✓ Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(6).
> ✓ Fed. R. Evid. 401.
> ✓ Fed. R. App. P. 2.  Fed. R. Crim. P. 42(a).
> ✓ Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 90 (A.L.I. 1981).
> ✓ Restatement (Second) of Torts § 90 cmt. a (A.L.I. 1965).
> ✓ U.C.C. § 2-202 (Unif. L. Comm'n 1977).
> ✓ U.S. Sent'g Guidelines Manual § 2D1.1(c) (U.S. Sent'g Comm'n 2004).
> ✓ Model Rules of Pro. Conduct r. 1.15 cmt. (A.B.A. 1983).

- Rules in force take no date.
- Restatements take the publisher and year. Comments and illustrations follow the section: `§ 90 cmt. a`, `§ 2.1 cmt. c, illus. 2`.
- Short form: *id.* (`Id. § 92`) or the full cite again. No *supra* for restatements or model codes (B4).

## Regulations and agency materials (B14, Rule 14)

> ✓ 46 C.F.R. § 166.01 (2009).
> ✓ Federal Acquisition Regulations for National Aeronautics and Space Administration, 55 Fed. Reg. 52,782 (Dec. 21, 1990) (to be codified at 48 C.F.R. pt. 1).
> ✓ Cal. Code Regs. tit. 2, § 22999 (2024).
> ✓ Trojan Transp., Inc., 249 NLRB 642 (1980). (An agency adjudication, named for the first private party.)

- The C.F.R. takes its year. A Federal Register cite takes the rule's common name, volume, first page, and exact date.
- Short form: `Id. § 10.13(c)(1).` or `50 C.F.R. § 10.12.` / `§ 10.12.`

## Legislative materials (B13, Rule 13)

> ✓ H.R. Rep. No. 99-253, pt. 1, at 54 (1985).
> ✓ S. Rep. No. 84-2, at 7 (1955).
> ✓ Protection from Personal Intrusion Act, H.R. 2448, 105th Cong. § 2(a) (1997).
> ✓ *Toxic Substances Control Act: Hearings on S. 776 Before the Subcomm. on the Env't of the S. Comm. on Com.*, 94th Cong. 343 (1975).
> ✓ 145 Cong. Rec. H1817 (daily ed. Apr. 12, 1999) (statement of Rep. Edward Pease).
> ✓ H.B. 636, 1999 Gen. Assemb., 413th Sess. (Md. 1999).

- Reports take "at" before the page, because the report number would otherwise run into it (Rule 3.2(a)).
- Ordinals in citations are `2d`, `3d`, `93d`, `103d`, never `2nd` or `93rd` (Rule 6.2(b)(ii)).
- Hearing titles are italicized. Bills, reports, and the Congressional Record are not.
- *Supra* is allowed for hearings, but not for bills, reports, or debates (B4).

## Books and treatises (B15, Rule 15)

Volume (if any), author's full name as printed, *title*, pincite, and a parenthetical with editor, edition, and year:

> ✓ Matthew Butterick, *Typography for Lawyers* 54 (2010).
> ✓ J.R. McNeill & William H. McNeill, *The Human Web* 319 (2003).
> ✓ David Hunter et al., *International Environmental Law and Policy* 555 (3d ed. 2006).
> ✓ 21 *Wright & Miller's Federal Practice & Procedure* § 1006 (3d ed. 1998).
> ✓ *Good-Faith Bargaining*, *Black's Law Dictionary* (10th ed. 2014).

- Two authors: `A & B`. Three: `A, B & C`. More than three: list them all, or the first followed by "et al."
- Short form: *id.* if it's the immediately preceding authority, otherwise *supra*: `Posner, *supra*, at 204–05.` Practitioner documents have no footnotes, so there is no "note" number (B15.2). Two works by the same author: `Llewellyn, *Remarks*, *supra*, at 401–06.` (B16.2 tip).

## Articles (B16, Rule 16)

> ✓ Fred R. Shapiro & Michelle Pearse, *The Most-Cited Law Review Articles of All Time*, 110 Mich. L. Rev. 1483, 1489 (2012).
> ✓ Natalie Cotton, Comment, *The Competence of Students as Editors of Law Reviews*, 154 U. Pa. L. Rev. 951, 982 n.104 (2006).
> ✓ Abigail Sullivan Moore, *This Is Your Brain on Drugs*, N.Y. Times, Oct. 29, 2014, at A1.

- Abbreviate the journal name per tables T6 and T13. Periodical abbreviations close up single capitals except a capital that names an institution: `Geo. L.J.`, `B.C. L. Rev.`, `N.Y.U. L. Rev.` (Rule 6.1(a)).
- Magazines and newspapers take the exact date and "at" before the page.
- Short form: *id.* or *supra*, as for books.

## Letters, interviews, and forthcoming works (Rule 17)

Give enough for the reader to know what it is and where it is kept. Copy dates and titles from the document itself:

> ✓ Letter from Graham Sternberg, Bluebook Ed. & Strategy Chair, Harv. L. Rev., to author (Feb. 13, 2020) (on file with author).
> ✓ Telephone Interview with Michael Leiter, President, Harv. L. Rev. (Oct. 22, 1999).

A forthcoming article takes the volume and journal, with `(forthcoming <month year>)` in place of the first page and year.

## Websites (B18, Rule 18.2)

Author, *title of the page*, main site title (abbreviated per T6 and T10), date and time if given, then the URL:

> ✓ David Lat, *Movie Night with Justice Breyer*, Above the L. (Oct. 27, 2014, at 15:31 ET), https://abovethelaw.com/2014/10/movie-night-with-justice-breyer/.
> ✓ Yahoo! Home Page, http://www.yahoo.com (last visited Mar. 18, 2020).

- Cite the print, official, or authenticated version when there is one (Rule 18.2). An exact PDF copy of a printed source is cited as the print source.
- If both HTML and a paginated PDF exist, cite the PDF and give a pincite (B18.1.1 tip).
- No date on the page: add `(last visited …)` after the URL.
- Short form: `Lat, *supra*.` or *id.* (B18.2).

## AI output (Rule 18.3, new in the 22d edition)

Rule 18.3 governs **citing** AI output. It doesn't make AI output an authority for what the law is. Cite the case, statute, or record itself.

- **A chatbot's response:** the prompt's author, the model with its version, the exact prompt in quotation marks, the date, and where the saved PDF is kept. Save a screenshot of the output as a PDF. A share link is not enough.

  > ✓ Luke Cronin, Google Gemini Advanced, "Who would make a better Supreme Court Justice: Beyoncé or Taylor Swift?" (Mar. 29, 2024) (on file with the Columbia Law Review).
  > ✗ Luke Cronin, Gemini Advanced, "Who would make a better Supreme Court Justice: Beyoncé or Taylor Swift?" (Mar. 29, 2024), https://g.co/gemini/share/425174222688.

- **Search results**, including a Westlaw search: the engine, the exact query, the number of results, the date, where the PDF is kept, and any filters: `Bing, "The Bluebook", 6,050,000 results (May 22, 2024) (on file with the Harvard Law Review).`
- **AI-generated content**, such as an image: cite it under the rule for its kind, adding `(generated by DALL-E 3)` or the like.

## Services (B19)

Volume, abbreviated title, publisher, subdivision, and date. For a case, the court goes in the date parenthetical:

> ✓ *In re Smithfield Est., Inc.*, [1985–1986 Transfer Binder] Bankr. L. Rep. (CCH) ¶ 70,707 (Bankr. D.R.I. Aug. 9, 1985).

## Foreign, international, tribal, and archival sources (B20–B23)

These aren't covered in depth here. In outline:

- **Foreign** (B20, Rule 20): end the citation with the jurisdiction in parentheses: `Emergency Powers Act (Act No. 3/1976) (Ir.).`
- **International** (B21, Rule 21): treaties take the name, parties, date, and treaty source: `U.N. Convention on the Law of the Sea, Dec. 10, 1982, 1833 U.N.T.S. 397.` International cases pincite paragraphs when available.
- **Tribal** (B22, Rule 22): for submission to a Tribal Nation, use that Nation's format. Otherwise use the Nation's own format plus a Rule 22 parallel cite, or Rule 22 alone if it has none. Don't abbreviate the Nation's name.
- **Archival** (B23, Rule 23): author, title, institution, date, and a parenthetical giving the archive, collection, and location.

Look up the specific rule before writing one of these. Don't extrapolate from the case rules.

## *Supra* and *hereinafter* (B4, Rule 4.2)

- **Allowed** for hearings, court filings, books, pamphlets, reports, unpublished materials, nonprint resources, periodicals, services, treaties, intergovernmental documents, and internal cross-references.
- **Not allowed** for cases, statutes, constitutions, other legislative materials, restatements, model codes, or regulations, except in extraordinary circumstances, such as an extremely long name.
- `[hereinafter …]` sets up a custom short name for an authority that is cumbersome or confusing to cite with plain *supra*. It goes right after the full citation, before any explanatory parenthetical, in the same typeface as the full name: `… (1977) [hereinafter *Hearings*] (statement of Prof. Wayne LaFave).` Don't use it when plain *supra* works.

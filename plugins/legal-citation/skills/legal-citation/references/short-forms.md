# Short citations and Id.

This file covers cases. For statutes and constitutions see `statutes-and-other-sources.md`. For the record and court documents see `court-documents.md`.

## When you may shorten

**Briefs and memos (Bluepages, B10.2).** After citing a case in full, later citations to it may use a short form when all three hold:

1. the reader can tell which case you mean,
2. the full citation appears in the same general discussion, and
3. the reader can find the full citation without trouble.

In a long memo or brief, if the case hasn't appeared for several pages or you have started a new major section, repeating the full citation is the safer choice. That is a judgment call, not a rule.

**Law review footnotes (Whitepages, Rule 10.9(a)).** A short form is allowed only if the case is already cited in the **same footnote**, or in **one of the preceding five footnotes** (in full or short form, including *id.*). Otherwise give the full citation again. This is a counting rule, so check it mechanically.

> Footnote 3 cites *Martinez-Fuerte* in full. Footnote 8 may use `See Martinez-Fuerte, 428 U.S. at 550.` Footnote 9 may too, because the footnote-8 short form counts. Footnote 15, with no citation to it since footnote 9, needs the full citation.

## Short-form patterns (B10.2, Rule 10.9)

| Form | When |
|---|---|
| `Bonner, 643 A.2d at 15.` | The standard short form. |
| `643 A.2d at 15.` | Your sentence already names the case, so the reader has no doubt which one. |
| `Id. at 15.` | Same case as the immediately preceding citation, different page. |
| `Id.` | Same case **and** same page or pages as the immediately preceding citation. |
| `Bonner, 643 A.2d 13.` | The whole case rather than a particular page: first page, no "at", no court-and-year parenthetical. |
| `Clark, 1991 WL 55402, at *3.` | A Westlaw, Lexis, or Bloomberg case: the database number replaces the reporter. |
| `Sam, slip op. at 12.` | A slip opinion (Rule 10.9(a)(iii)). |
| `Chalfin, 426 Pa. at 465, 233 A.2d at 563.` | A case cited with parallel reporters: a pincite for each. |

Every short form that points to a page uses "at". The case name is italicized (or underlined) in both styles, and the comma after it is not.

## Which party name to use (B10.2, Rule 10.9(a)(i))

Use the first party's name unless it is a geographical unit, a government entity or official, or another common litigant ("State," "United States," "People," "Commonwealth," a city, "Commissioner"). In that case use the other party.

- `State v. Bonner` → `Bonner`
- `United States v. Brownlee` → `Brownlee`
- `NAACP v. Alabama ex rel. Patterson` → `Patterson`, not `NAACP` or `Alabama`
- `Reno v. Bossier Par. Sch. Bd.` → `Bossier Par. Sch. Bd.`, not `Reno`

A long name may be shortened if the result is still unambiguous: `First Nat'l Trust & Inv. Corp.` → `First Nat'l`. Keep a procedural phrase that is part of the name you use: `In re Draughon Training, 119 B.R. at 930.`

## Id. (B4, B10.2, Rule 4.1)

- **Id.** refers to the immediately preceding authority, and only when that citation contained exactly one authority. After `See Coyle, 567 A.2d at 873; Bonner, 643 A.2d at 15.`, the next citation cannot be `Id.`. The reader can't tell which case it means.
- Authorities that appear only inside an explanatory parenthetical (`(quoting …)`, `(citing …)`), an explanatory phrase (*quoted in*), or prior or subsequent history don't count toward that rule.
- `Id.` alone means the same page or pages. For a new page: `Id. at 874.`
- Capitalize it only when it starts a citation sentence: `Id. at 874.` In a citation clause or after a signal it is lowercase: `see id. at 874`, `See id.`
- **The period is part of the abbreviation and is italicized (or underlined) with it.**
- **A different opinion in the same case must be named** (Rule 10.9(b)(i)): after `Youngstown, 343 U.S. at 584`, write `Id. at 635 (Jackson, J., concurring).` To go back to the majority, write `Id. at 582 (majority opinion).` After an intervening citation to another source, the case is presumed to mean the majority again.
- **Parallel citations** (Rule 10.9(b)(ii)): `Id. at 465, 233 A.2d at 563.` Not `Id. at 465.` and not `Id. at 465, 563.`
- **Law review footnotes:** *id.* may also refer to the previous footnote, if that footnote cited exactly one authority (Rule 4.1).
- *Id.* works for statutes, regulations, books, and articles too, but **not for the record unless it saves significant space** (B17.2). See `court-documents.md`.
- **Never use *supra* for cases** (B4, Rule 4.2). The one exception: a case with an established `[hereinafter]` name in a law review footnote, when the name is extremely long.

## Referring to a case in your text (B10.1.1(vi), Rule 10.9(c))

Once a case has been cited in full in the same general discussion, your prose may refer to it by one party's name with no citation: "The court in *Bonner* found …" Use the party you would use in the short form, not the government party. Example from B10.1.1(vi): ✓ "… formulation in *Penn Central*." ✗ "… formulation in *City of New York*."

## Check the sequence

Short forms are positional, so check them in document order. Track the immediately preceding authority as you go, and in footnotes, which footnote last cited each case.

```
… State v. Coyle, 567 A.2d 870, 873 (Del. Super. Ct. 1989).      full citation
… Coyle, 567 A.2d at 871–72.                                       short form
… Id.                                                              still 871–72
… Id. at 874.                                                      new page
… Hernandez, 573 A.3d at 218.                                      different case: no Id.
… Coyle, 567 A.2d at 874.                                          back to Coyle: short form, not Id.
```

Flag:

- an `Id.` that follows a citation containing more than one authority,
- an `Id.` that follows a citation to a different case,
- an `Id.` that switches to a concurrence or dissent, or back to the majority, without saying so,
- a short form for a case that has no earlier full citation,
- in a law review, a short form more than five footnotes after the last citation to the case,
- a short form missing its reporter (`Bonner, 643 at 15`) or its "at",
- a *supra* for a case.

# Case citations

## Anatomy of a full citation (B10.1, Rule 10)

```
State v. Bonner, 643 A.2d 13, 15 (Del. Super. Ct. 1995).
```

| Part | Here | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Case name | `State v. Bonner` | Italicized (or underlined) in a brief or memo. The comma after it is **not**. In a law review footnote's full citation it is roman (see `typeface-and-placement.md`). |
| Volume | `643` | |
| Reporter | `A.2d` | Spacing matters. See below. |
| First page | `13` | The page the opinion begins on. |
| Pincite | `15` | The page you are relying on, after a comma. |
| Court and year | `(Del. Super. Ct. 1995)` | One parenthetical. |
| Other parentheticals | | Weight of authority, then quoting or citing, then explanatory (`signals-and-parentheticals.md`) |
| Subsequent history | `, aff'd, …` | See "Prior and subsequent history" below. |

## Pincites and page ranges (B10.1.2, Rule 3.2)

- **A pincite is required** for every citation except one introduced by *see generally* (Rule 1.2(f)).
- Separate the first page and the pincite with a comma: `843 A.2d 136, 138`. Writing `843 A.2d 136–37` merges them into what looks like a range and drops the first page.
- If the material is on the first page, repeat it: `843 A.2d 136, 136`. A range that starts there: `843 A.2d 136, 136–37`.
- Ranges take an en dash (a hyphen is acceptable). Keep the last two digits and drop any other repeated digits: `871–72`, `217–18`, `1065–69`, `99–100`, `1020–30`.
- Separate pages that aren't consecutive with commas: `567 A.2d at 871, 874`, `103, 106–08, 132`.
- **Footnotes:** `573 A.3d at 217 n.11`, with no space after `n.`. Several notes: `141 nn.180–86`. A text page and a note that starts on it: `123 & n.90`. A footnote that spans pages: cite the page where it begins. A range plus a note within it: `1523–24, 1524 n.75`.
- **A separate opinion:** pincite the page you rely on after the first page of the *case*, not the first page of the concurrence or dissent: `369 U.S. 186, 297 (1962) (Frankfurter, J., dissenting)`.
- When a point recurs throughout and listing pages is impractical, use *passim* in place of a pincite, with no comma before it.
- Never use "p." or "pp." in a citation.

## U.S. Supreme Court: one reporter only (B10.1.3(i), Rule 10.3.1)

Cite only U.S. Reports when the case appears there. Drop the parallel Supreme Court Reporter and Lawyers' Edition cites that database "copy with reference" features add:

- Wrong: `Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436, 444, [S. Ct. cite], [L. Ed. cite] (1966)`
- Right: `Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436, 444 (1966)`

Use `S. Ct.` only for decisions not yet in U.S. Reports. The court parenthetical then still has the year only.

## State cases: which reporter (B10.1.3(iv)–(v), Rule 10.3)

- **Memos, law reviews, and federal filings:** cite the regional reporter (`A.2d`, `N.W.2d`, …) if the case is there. Otherwise cite the state's official reporter.
- **Documents filed in a state court:** cite whatever that court's local rules require. Many require the official state reporter followed by a parallel regional citation, with a pincite for each. Drop the state or court from the parenthetical when the official reporter makes it clear (B10.1.3, BT2):

  > ✓ `Kenford Co. v. County of Erie, 73 N.Y.2d 312, 537 N.E.2d 176, 540 N.Y.S.2d 1 (1989).`
  > ✓ `Chalfin v. Specter, 426 Pa. 464, 465, 233 A.2d 562, 563 (1967).`

- **Public domain (medium-neutral) citations:** if the jurisdiction issues one, give it, and also give the regional reporter if the case is there (Rule 10.3.1(b), Rule 10.3.3). Pincite the paragraph:

  > ✓ `Beck v. Beck, 1999 ME 110, ¶ 6, 733 A.2d 981, 983.`
  > ✓ `State v. Brennan, 1998-NMCA-176, ¶ 7, 126 N.M. 389, 970 P.2d 161.`

  Follow the state's own format when it differs (T1). Never construct a public domain number.

## Unreported cases: Westlaw, Lexis, slip opinions (B10.1.4, Rule 10.8.1)

```
<Case name>, No. <docket number>, <year> WL <number>, at *<page> (<court> <Mon. day, year>).
```

> ✓ `Kvaas Constr. Co. v. United States, No. 90-266C, 1991 WL 47632, at *2–3 (Cl. Ct. Apr. 8, 1991).`
> ✓ `Albrecht v. Stranczek, No. 87 C 9535, 1991 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 5088, at *1, *3 (N.D. Ill. Apr. 15, 1991).`
> ✓ `Brown v. Henderson Cnty. Sheriff's Off., No. 1:23-cv-00270-MR-WCM, 2024 LX 18483, at *1 (W.D.N.C. May 14, 2024).`

- **Docket number:** give it as it appears on the court's documents, not as a database stripped it (`615CV1824ORL41GJK` is a database's rendering of `6:15-cv-1824-Orl-41GJK`). You may omit an initial digit before a colon and judge's initials at the end. Omit nothing else (Rule 10.8.1(a)). Always give the full number: `No. 75-31`, not `No. 31`. For several numbers, use `Nos.`, and the lead-in may be dropped after the first (B10.1.4 tip).
- **Database identifier:** `WL` (Westlaw), `U.S. Dist. LEXIS` / `U.S. App. LEXIS` or the newer Lexis form `LX` (Rule 10.8.1(a) treats them as interchangeable), `BL` (Bloomberg Law). Copy it exactly.
- **Star pages:** `at *8`. For a range, drop the star on the second number: `at *2–3`, not `at *2–*3`. Keep the star on each page that isn't consecutive: `at *1, *3` (Rule 3.2(a)).
- **Date:** the parenthetical takes the **full date** (abbreviated month, day, year), not just the year (Rule 10.5(b)). Months follow T12 (`Sep.`, not `Sept.`).
- **Federal Appendix and other "unpublished" cases that are in a reporter** take the year only: `United States v. Tando, 68 F. App'x 85 (9th Cir. 2003).` (Rule 10.5(b)).
- **Slip opinion** (no database): `Groucho Marx Prods. v. Playboy Enters., No. 77 Civ. 1782 (S.D.N.Y. Dec. 30, 1977).` A page: `No. 90-345, slip op. at 6 (D. Mass. Dec. 4, 1990).`
- **Available only on a court's website:** you may add the URL at the end (B10.1.4(iii)).
- **Pending case:** date of the most recent major disposition, labeled: `(D. Mass. filed Sep. 18, 1990)`, `(1st Cir. argued Jan. 10, 1991)` (Rule 10.5(c)).
- Short form: `<Short name>, <year> WL <number>, at *<page>.`, then `Id. at *5.` When the first party is a government official, the short form uses the other party: `Sec'y of Lab. v. Smith Co.` becomes `Smith Co.`

## The court-and-year parenthetical (B10.1.3, Rule 10.4, Rule 10.5)

Name the court only to the extent the reporter doesn't already make it clear.

| Court | How it appears |
|---|---|
| U.S. Supreme Court, in U.S. Reports or the Supreme Court Reporter | Year only: `Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436, 444 (1966).` |
| Federal courts of appeals | `(3d Cir. 2006)`, `(D.C. Cir. 1992)`, `(Fed. Cir. 2015)`. Ordinals are `2d` and `3d`, never `2nd`, `3rd`, `CA3`, or `C.C.A.` |
| Federal district courts | `(S.D.N.Y. 2010)`, `(D. Mass. 2015)`, `(N.D. Ill. 2008)`. Give the district, never the division: `(C.D. Cal. 2017)`, not `(C.D. Cal. E.D. 2017)`. |
| A state's highest court | State only: `(Mich. 1999)`, not `(Mich. Sup. Ct. 1999)`. If the reporter is that state's official reporter for its highest court, give only the year: `Bates v. Tappan, 99 Mass. 376 (1868).` |
| Lower state courts | State and court: `(Del. Super. Ct. 1995)`, `(Pa. Super. Ct. 1992)`, `(Minn. Ct. App. 1995)` |
| Lower state courts whose reporter already names the court | Omit what the reporter conveys: a New York Appellate Division case in `N.Y.S.2d` is `(App. Div. 1985)`, not `(N.Y. App. Div. 1985)`. A California Court of Appeal case in `Cal. Rptr. 2d` is `(Ct. App. 1999)`. |

- Don't give the department, district, or county of an intermediate state court unless it matters to the point (B10.1.3(v), Rule 10.4(b)): `(Sup. Ct. N.Y. Cnty. 1944)`, `(Tex. App. 14th 2003)`.
- **Year:** the year of decision, not the term. If it is ambiguous, follow the reporter's running head (Rule 10.5(a)).
- **Same-year history:** when a later disposition in the history came the same year, the year appears only once, after the last decision that year: `United States v. Eller, 114 F. Supp. 284 (M.D.N.C.), rev'd, 208 F.2d 716 (4th Cir. 1953).` (Rule 10.5(d), Rule 10.7.1(a)).

**Never strip periods from a court abbreviation.** It is `N.Y.`, not `NY`. Some court databases store display names without periods. Adding missing punctuation can fix a citation; removing it never does. Court abbreviations are in `abbreviations.md`.

## Reporter spacing (B6, Rule 6.1(a))

- Run adjacent single capitals together. Numbers and ordinals count as single capitals: `A.2d`, `F.3d`, `F.4th`, `N.W.2d`, `U.S.`, `S.D.N.Y.`, `N.Y.S.2d`.
- Put a space next to any abbreviation of two or more letters: `F. Supp. 2d`, `So. 2d`, `S. Ct.`, `L. Ed. 2d`, `Cal. App. 4th`, `F. App'x`, `D. Mass.`
- Wrong in a memo or law review: `F.Supp.2d`, `F.App'x`, `So.2d`, `S.Ct.`, `L.Ed.2d`.
- **Exception:** in a document filed with a court that imposes a word limit, B6 lets you close up reporter abbreviations (`S.Ct.`, `F.Supp.2d`) to save words. If a brief does that consistently, don't flag it. Flag a mix of closed and spaced forms.

The common reporters are listed in `abbreviations.md`.

## Case names (B10.1.1, Rule 10.2)

Start from the name printed at the beginning of the opinion in the reporter you cite. Database captions and running headers are not in citation form; a Westlaw header may read `U.S. v. Brownlee`. Then:

- **Keep only the first party on each side.** Drop "et al.", additional parties, and alternative names (`a.k.a.`, `d/b/a`). For consolidated cases, cite only the first action. Keep the first-listed relator and every word of a partnership name: `Eisen v. Spradlin, Lincoln & Amorosi`.
- **Individuals: surnames only.** Drop first names, middle names, and initials: `Darwin v. Dawkins`. Keep a surname of several words whole (`Van der Velt`). For names entirely in a language that gives the surname first, keep the full name (`Yao Zhen Guang`). For Spanish- or Portuguese-derived names, keep the surname and all names after it (`Ortega y Gasset`). Keep given names in business names (`Tanya Bartucz, Inc.`).
- **Drop a leading "The"** from a party's name, except in an in rem case or "The King"/"The Queen": `Seattle Times v. Univ. of Wash.`
- **Procedural phrases:** "in the matter of," "petition of," and "application of" become `In re`. "On the relation of," "on behalf of," and "as next friend of" become `ex rel.` With adversary parties, keep only `ex rel.`: `Gorman v. Bruh`, not `In re Gorman v. Bruh`. Keep `Ex parte`. Keep descriptive phrases like "Estate of" and "Will of": `In re Will of Holt`. Procedural phrases are always italicized, even in a roman case name.
- **Descriptive terms for a named party** (administrator, executor, trustee, appellee) are dropped: `Burns v. McMillen`, not `Burns v. McMillen, Administrator`.
- **Business designations:** drop `Inc.`, `Ltd.`, `L.L.C.`, `N.A.`, and the like when the name also contains `Co.`, `Corp.`, `Ass'n`, `Bros.`, `Ins.`, or `R.R.`: `Wis. Packing Co. v. Ind. Refrigerator Lines, Inc.` Read this narrowly. Keep the designation if the name could otherwise be mistaken for a person's.
- **States as parties:** drop "State of," "Commonwealth of," and "People of": `Doe v. Commonwealth of Pennsylvania` becomes `Doe v. Pennsylvania`. When a state prosecutes in its own courts, it appears simply as `State`, `Commonwealth`, or `People`. Hence `State v. Bonner` in a Delaware court.
- **Cities and counties:** drop "City of," "County of," and similar words unless they begin the party's name: `Mayor of New York v. Clinton`, but `Butts v. City of New York`. Drop prepositional phrases of location not following "City" and the like (`Surrick v. Board of Wardens`), unless that would leave a one-word name or the place is part of a business's full name (`Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey`). Drop "of America" after "United States," and drop geographic designations that follow a comma (`City of Arlington v. FCC`).
- **"United States" is never abbreviated when it is the whole party name:** `United States v. Brownlee`, never `U.S. v. Brownlee`. "U.S." is fine as part of a longer name: `U.S. Dep't of Agric.`
- **Spell out any geographic name that is a party's entire name:** `Delaware v. Prouse`, not `Del. v. Prouse`.
- **Commissioner of Internal Revenue** is `Commissioner`, and `Comm'r` in a citation.
- **Very long names:** omit words not needed to identify the case. The reporter's running head is a guide (Rule 10.2.1).
- **A case known by a popular name** may show it in italic parentheses after the reporter name: `Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer (Steel Seizure), 343 U.S. 579 (1952).` Don't replace the reporter name with it (Rule 10.2.1(k)).

### Abbreviating words in a case name depends on where the name appears

| Where the name appears | What to abbreviate |
|---|---|
| **Inside a citation**: a citation sentence, citation clause, or footnote citation | Every word in T6 (`abbreviations.md`), **even the first word of a party's name**, and every geographic unit in T10, unless the unit is the party's entire name. Other words of eight or more letters may be abbreviated if that saves substantial space and stays unambiguous. Entities known by their initials may use them: `NLRB`, `FCC` (Rule 10.2.2, Rule 6.1(b)). |
| **In a textual sentence**: the name is part of your prose | Only widely recognized initialisms (NAACP, FCC) and eight words: `&`, `Ass'n`, `Bros.`, `Co.`, `Corp.`, `Inc.`, `Ltd.`, `No.` Do not abbreviate even these when they are the first word of a party's name (B10.1.1(vi), Rule 10.2.1(c)). |

The same case, two ways:

- Prose: In *Smith v. Department of Social Services*, the court held …
- Citation: *Smith v. Dep't of Soc. Servs.*, [volume] [reporter] [page] (…).

> ✓ In *Penn Central Transportation Co. v. City of New York*, 366 N.E.2d 1271 (N.Y. 1977), the court applied …
> ✗ In *Penn Cent. Transp. Co. v. City of New York*, 366 N.E.2d 1271 (N.Y. 1977), the court applied …
> ✓ … *See, e.g.*, *S. Pac. Co. v. Jensen*, 244 U.S. 205, 225–26 (1917) (Pitney, J., dissenting).
> ✗ … *See* *Southern Pacific Co. v. Jensen*, 244 U.S. 205, 225–26 (1917). (Not abbreviated in a citation.)

Form a plural by adding "s" to the abbreviation: `Servs.`, `Dep'ts`, `Ass'ns`.

## Prior and subsequent history (B10.1.6, Rule 10.7)

A full citation includes the case's **subsequent** history, introduced by an italicized phrase from T8 (`abbreviations.md`), with a roman comma after it. Give **prior** history only when it matters to your point or the cited decision is unintelligible without it.

> ✓ `Gucci Am., Inc. v. Gold Ctr. Jewelry, 997 F. Supp. 399 (S.D.N.Y. 1998), rev'd, 158 F.3d 631 (2d Cir.).`
> ✓ `Kubrick v. United States, 581 F.2d 1092 (3d Cir. 1978), aff'g 435 F. Supp. 166 (E.D. Pa. 1977), rev'd, 444 U.S. 111 (1979).` (Prior history comes first.)

**Omit:**

- **denials of certiorari** or of similar discretionary review, unless the denial is **less than two years old** or particularly relevant,
- history on remand and denials of rehearing, unless relevant to your point,
- a disposition later withdrawn by the same court.

> ✗ `Cent. Ill. Pub. Serv. Co. v. Westervelt, 342 N.E.2d 463 (Ill. App. Ct. 1976), aff'd, 367 N.E.2d 661 (Ill. 1977), cert. denied, 434 U.S. 1070 (1978).` (A 1978 cert. denial is long past two years.)

**Always note** that a case was:

- **overruled**, explicitly, by the same court or a higher one: `Nat'l League of Cities v. Usery, 426 U.S. 833 (1976), overruled by Garcia v. San Antonio Metro. Transit Auth., 469 U.S. 528 (1985).`
- **abrogated**, meaning effectively but not explicitly overruled: `abrogated by …`
- **superseded** by a statute or constitutional amendment enacted to reverse it. Cite the statute, ideally to the session laws, and optionally a later case recognizing it: `superseded by statute, Civil Rights Act of 1991, Pub. L. No. 102-166, 105 Stat. 1074, as recognized in …`

Other mechanics:

- **Name change on appeal:** give the new name after *sub nom.*, even for a small change: `rev'd sub nom. Leroy v. Great W. United Corp., 443 U.S. 173 (1979).` Skip it when the parties are merely reversed, when the history is a cert. or rehearing denial, or when the change is only stylistic ("State" to "California").
- **A disposition without its usual significance** gets a reason: *vacated as moot*, *appeal dismissed per stipulation*.
- **Order:** explanatory parentheticals come **before** the history: `…, 1530–31 (9th Cir. 1991) (holding …), rev'd en banc, 963 F.2d 1220 (9th Cir. 1992).`
- **History of history** chains with another phrase: `73 F.R.D. 387 (S.D.N.Y.), rev'd, 568 F.2d 974 (2d Cir. 1977), rev'd, 441 U.S. 153 (1979).` Several dispositions by the same court are joined with italic *and*.
- **Never invent history.** It comes from a citator (KeyCite, Shepard's) or the later opinion itself. If you can't check it, say the history is unverified.
